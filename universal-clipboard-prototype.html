      Transfers

      Long transfers play faster than real time here

        Your devices connect directly whenever a route exists. A relay carries encrypted traffic only when nothing else works, and it never sees what you copy.

        Finding a route

        Wi-Fi, Tailscale and relay are tried at once, and the fastest wins

        Same Wi-Fi

        Devices announce themselves with mDNS (the same family as Bonjour) and connect straight to each other. No account, no internet, works on a plane.

        2 to 9 ms

        Tailscale

        The app treats your tailnet as one more network. It reads the peer list from Tailscale, then connects over the 100.x addresses. Tailscale does the NAT traversal and falls back to its own relays when a phone sits behind carrier NAT.

        20 to 50 ms

        Relay

        An optional rendezvous server forwards ciphertext over WebSocket when two devices cannot reach each other any other way. It holds no keys. Skip it entirely if you only use Wi-Fi and Tailscale.

        55 to 130 ms

        Apple Continuity

        Only between the MacBook and the iPhone. Apple's own software carries it over Bluetooth and its private Wi-Fi link, so we do not touch it. The MacBook app watches the result and passes it on.

        Felt instant

        The iPhone, without an iPhone app

        Apple already syncs Mac and iPhone, so the MacBook becomes the bridge
        1. iPhone to everything else

          You copy on the iPhone. Apple delivers it to the MacBook clipboard. Our Mac app sees the change and sends it to Windows, Android and Linux.

        2. Everything else to iPhone

          Copy on Windows. Our Mac app writes it to the MacBook clipboard. Apple then lets you paste it on the iPhone as usual.

        The catch. The MacBook has to be awake and near the iPhone. If it sleeps or leaves, the iPhone drops out of your circle until it is back. Try the "MacBook asleep" switch on the Prototype tab. A small iPhone app for those moments comes later.

        • Tested

          A copy on a real iPhone showed up on the Mac clipboard, tagged by Apple as coming from another device. That tag lets us avoid echo loops.

        • Tested

          Text written to the Mac clipboard by a background program pasted instantly on the iPhone.

        • To check

        What happens when you copy

        1. Notice the copy

          Each desktop app watches its clipboard through the system's own change notification, so there is no polling delay.

        2. Filter

          Hash the content to avoid echo loops. Skip anything the password manager flagged as concealed.

        3. Push small items straight away

          Text, rich text and images up to about 8 MB go out immediately, so the paste on the other device is instant.

        4. Promise big items

          Copied files send only a name and size. The bytes stream when you paste, straight from the device that copied them.

        Trust and security

        1. Pair once

          Scan a QR code or type a six digit code. Each device creates a keypair and the two exchange public keys.

        2. Mutual handshake every time

          Connections use a Noise handshake over QUIC. Both sides prove their key before a single byte of clipboard moves.

        3. Your circle only

          Unknown devices are ignored, not prompted. Devices in your circle can send you files without asking, and you can switch that off.

        4. Revoke anywhere

          Remove a device from any other device and it drops out of the circle immediately.

        Build order

        1. Phase 1

          Rust core and the Mac and Windows apps. Text and images sync on the same Wi-Fi. The Mac app is the iPhone bridge, so the iPhone works from day one.

        2. Phase 2

          Direct file streams with progress, resume and checksums. Drag a file onto a device.

        3. Phase 3

          Android app, with the send tile and share target.

        4. Phase 4

          iPhone app with a share sheet and keyboard, for when the MacBook is asleep or away.

        5. Phase 5

          Tailscale routes, the optional relay, searchable history and per-device rules.
